About Ian

I'm Ian, a Unity gameplay programmer from João Pessoa, Brazil. Across a Game Development degree I worked on three team games as a programmer — owning player controllers, animation, combat, enemy AI, and the architecture that holds those systems together. The most recent, Dark Side of Horror, is a 4-player co-op shooter my team shipped publicly on itch.io; it was the final project of the Tecnólogo em Jogos Digitais (Game Development Associate Degree) at PUC Minas.

My journey

Across those three games I kept gravitating to the same work: the systems a player touches every second — how a character moves and animates, how a weapon responds, how an enemy decides to chase. I like owning a system end to end, from the rough first pass to the version that finally feels right.

Engineering Breakdown

The same three games, up close — the systems I designed and built, and the stack behind each. Reverse chronological.

Lead Programmer

Dark Side of Horror · 2025 · Team of 6

Owned gameplay programming and the systems architecture across the project.

  • Built the player controller end to end — movement, rotation, Animator integration, a stamina system (drain/regen/cooldown), and weapon-hand IK via Animation Rigging's TwoBoneIK constraint.
  • Designed a data-driven weapon framework — five weapon types (Pistol, BurstRifle, MiniGun, Shotgun, Sniper), two fire modes (Single, Auto), burst toggle, spread, and Pack-A-Punch upgrade tiers — all from shared ScriptableObject definitions.
  • Built the perk system end to end (perk machines, perk effects feeding back into movement and stats).
  • Built the co-op down-and-revive flow with multi-player input handoff on respawn.
  • Wrote a generic object pool keyed by prefab, reused for bullets, pickups, and VFX.
  • Rebuilt the wave director with a Black Ops 2-style zombie-count formula and health curve, ranged-enemy progression, per-zombie speed-tier distribution, and a mid-match respawn flow.

Gameplay Programmer — state machines & lock-on

Feneco · 2024 · Team of 6

Owned the state-machine architecture and the lock-on targeting system.

  • Built finite state machines for the player and enemies, sharing abstract base-state classes — six player states (FreeLook, Targeting, Jumping, Falling, Attacking, Hanging) and six enemy states (Idle, Patrol, Chase, Attack, Impact, Dead).
  • Built lock-on targeting — viewport-space target selection (closest to screen center) integrated with a Cinemachine TargetGroup.
  • Implemented NavMesh-driven enemy pursuit — the agent computes the path, the CharacterController applies motion.
  • Built an impact/knockback force layer (ForceReceiver) that composes external forces with character motion and temporarily disables the NavMeshAgent during knockback to prevent path-fighting.
  • Built a ledge-climb mechanic (LedgeDetector + PlayerHangingState) layered onto the state machine.
  • Built the inventory + equip system (gameplay logic and UI) from scratch.

Co-lead Gameplay Programmer

Survival-Chaos · 2023 · Team of 6

Co-led gameplay; owned the orbital motion model and the boss fight.

  • Designed a consistent orbital-motion convention — every actor (player, enemies, boss, projectiles) rotates around a shared "Scenario" anchor via transform.RotateAround, so the whole arena reads as one coherent cylinder.
  • Built a three-phase boss with three independently timed attack patterns and a laser phase that toggles via a player-position trigger zone.
  • Implemented enemy movement, three trigger-based collision handlers (two flip orbital direction on contact; the third toggles the boss laser based on player position), and the damage/health flow.
